# Local ancestral states: study before design

## NEW STUDY — 2026-10-09; previous authors' conclusions are not amended

[observation] Scope: estimate **allele-frequency change conditional on a local ancestral state**, then compare its direction for the **same state shared between populations**. Neither genome-wide ancestry regression, ancestry-enrichment scanning, nor clustering diploid SNP dosages is this estimand. This study preceded the new preregistration and any new outcome analysis.

URLs, retrieval time, named queries and content are retained in `data/derived/local_ancestral_state/literature/`; reproducible retrieval is `analysis/local_ancestral_state_study.py`. The search is targeted, not a systematic review. Two broad first searches retrieved irrelevant food/agricultural papers: these are not evidence. No external executable or package was installed.

## 1. What the methods actually infer

[inference] Three concepts must not be conflated: (i) **source-population local ancestry**, e.g. a chromosome segment resembling a farmer reference; (ii) **local haplotype-copying/sharing state**, a finer equivalence class in a fitted linked-marker model; (iii) **genealogical ancestor**, a node of a local tree with an estimated coalescence time. A source label does not identify a most recent ancestor. A HMM's “ancestral haplotype” is a statistical cluster, not a sequenced historical person. The resolution chosen determines which composition is held fixed.

[observation] Requirements and documented limitations (GL = genotype/read likelihood; ordinary called-genotype methods are not automatically GL-aware):

| Method and primary source | Input/reference requirement | Model and assumptions | Relevance and failure modes here |
|---|---|---|---|
| RFMix — Maples et al. (2013) | Labeled source reference haplotypes; modern common pipelines use phased biallelic target/reference genotypes and genetic maps. No standard ancient GL input. | Window random forests plus conditional random field; learns from admixed samples; source differentiation and representative references matter. | Strong empirical ancient benchmark, but current loader exposes no chromosome phase. | FLARE — Browning, Waples & Browning (2023) | Phased target and labeled reference haplotypes, map; no standard read-GL interface. | Extended Li–Stephens copying HMM with scalable reference compression/composite haplotypes. | Excellent large-reference scalability is NOT rescued by having 16,339 targets if each ancestral reference is tiny. Mies & Mathieson (2026) obtained zero hunter-gatherer ancestry; their discussion reports similar small-source failure in simulations. Not the first choice for the current ancient source panels. |

BIMBAM called allele pairs, not arbitrary ALT dosage-as-likelihood. No recombination map required. [observation] Guan (2014) explicitly says that source training data can be unphased, although computationally slower; a later MOSAIC comparison describes the usual phased-source use. The primary ELAI paper/manual is authoritative for supported unphased inputs. The official manual warns that falsely declaring an unphased panel phased is “very harmful.”

## 2. Ancient-specific evidence changes the recommendation

[observation] **Mies & Mathieson (2026), “Local ancestry inference identifies robust evidence of selection in Neolithic Europe,”** is directly applicable, rather than a continental-admixture analogy. In 176 imputed/phased Neolithic targets with 48 hunter-gatherers and 7 farmers, they filtered to 572,223 autosomal 1240K-overlap SNPs (MAF > .01, imputation quality > .8). Before source-size optimization, Gnomix's mean ancestry difference from qpAdm was .04 and other methods .21–.52. Local correlations in 100 kb bins between methods were only **.08–.50**; individual averages ranged **.15–.43, mean .32**, despite correlated global estimates. Their preferred RFMix/Gnomix tract fits were 10.3/16.4 generations; several other methods yielded implausibly short tracts. They replicated ancestry-deviation signals in independent sets of 378 and 1,121 targets, including data from Akbari. These are **ancestry-deviation**, not ancestry-specific allele-frequency time-series, analyses.

[observation] Their Discussion gives a particularly important distinction: observed FADS1/SLC24A5 frequency changes .23/.33 exceed mixture-based expectations .03/.06, but this supports either within-background change **or underestimated ancestry shifts**. It is not direct measurement of the requested conditional trajectory. HLA misclassification could resemble selection. Their explicit ground-truth limitation is that empirical agreement/global structure/tract expectations do not prove correct local segments.

[observation] **Bougiouri et al. (2026 preprint), “Promises and limitations of local ancestry inference in imputed ancient genomes,”** benchmarks RFMix/FLARE/MOSAIC/simpLAI under simulated ancient sampling, source divergence and admixture dates. Only its abstract and the discussion in Mies & Mathieson were read here; no unverified numerical result from its supplement is asserted. **Sousa da Mota et al. (2023), “Imputation of ancient human genomes,”** studies low-coverage imputation; its quality-dependent validation is a reason to retain genotype probabilities/quality and ancestry-matched assessment, not to equate complete hard calls with true observed chromosomes.

[inference] The diagnosis “AIMs give structure, all SNPs give coverage” is incomplete. Published haplotype methods model LD/copying or learn linked windows; they are not unweighted k-means on every dosage or a frequency-distance HMM whose confidence grows as if SNPs were independent. Reference imbalance, source drift, genotype uncertainty and model resolution must be solved as separate problems. Modern 1KG EUR is an imputation/donor resource, not an EHG/Iranian ancestral truth set.

## 3. Estimating frequencies conditional on ancestry is a separate step

[observation] **Zhang, Browning & Browning (2016), “ASAFE: ancestry-specific allele frequency estimation,”** derives EM estimates for a biallelic marker in three-way admixed samples given genotypes and the two local ancestries, **without knowing the phase of genotype relative to ancestry**. This is the relevant conceptual precedent. It does not infer ancestry; it assumes the supplied local ancestries. Its simulated accuracy does not establish robustness to ancient imputation or state misassignment.

[inference] For pair of local states (a,b) and unphased genotype G, a frequency likelihood is

`P(G=0|a,b)=(1-p_a)(1-p_b)`;
`P(G=1|a,b)=p_a(1-p_b)+(1-p_a)p_b`;
`P(G=2|a,b)=p_a p_b`.

[inference] With uncertain pair assignment `q_i(a,b)` from EXCLUDED-locus flanks, marginalize **inside** the likelihood, `L_i = sum_{a<=b} q_i(a,b) P(G_i|a,b)`, and with genuine read likelihoods use `sum_g GL_i(g) P(g|a,b)`. This soft-pair extension and the time-dependent `p_{a,pop}(t)` below are **our proposed extension**, not a claim that published ASAFE already does either. Dosage alone is generally insufficient to reconstruct three genotype probabilities. Upper ancestry dosage alone is also insufficient to reconstruct pair probabilities. Multiplying `G_i/2` by expected ancestry count incorrectly allocates heterozygous alleles.

[observation] **Atkinson et al. (2021), “Tractor uses local ancestry to enable the inclusion of admixed individuals in GWAS and to boost power,”** separates ancestry-specific allelic dosage and local ancestry in association models and includes phase-error correction. It illustrates why allelic assignment and ancestry assignment must be distinct. Its outcome is phenotype association, not temporal frequency change; its phased allele splitting cannot be copied onto unordered AMY H1/H2. Admixture mapping tests trait association with local ancestry; ancestry-enrichment scans test unusual ancestry fraction. Neither is the requested statistic.

[inference] A time-series analogue is to replace the constant frequency in the ASAFE-type convolution by a state/population-specific frequency path, while retaining the phase sum. Wright–Fisher/drift-aware temporal inference supplies a null for *change*; genealogy-based selection/trajectory inference supplies another route if trees and phase are available. Those frameworks do not automatically yield independent shared-state replicates. No retrieved paper validates this exact lower-haplotype-state, multi-population AMY time-series procedure; this extension requires calibration before biology.

## 4. References, sharing and identifiability

[observation] ELAI's Discussion explicitly defines local sharing as the probability that two haplotypes descend from the same **lower cluster**, reflecting relatedness. MOSAIC instead learns relationships of donor groups to unseen mixing sources. Local genealogy inference can dispense with named ancestral source panels but still needs sequence/phase information and model assumptions. Thus a *named curated population reference* is not logically necessary for a statistical sharing state; no-reference is not no-model, no-data, or no-uncertainty.

[inference] A reference-free single-upper-layer linked model (ELAI's S=1 limit is fastPHASE) can define lower-cluster sharing. It cannot label those clusters “WHG” or assign their MRCA ages without further evidence. Across separately fitted windows/runs, cluster indices are arbitrary and can split/merge. Across populations, fit a common donor/state dictionary, not one K-means/HMM per population followed by assuming label 1 matches label 1. Validation labels must be held out from state learning if they are to provide an independent known-structure test.

[inference] Unphased data do not make population frequency estimation universally impossible. Homo-ancestry pairs and variation in ancestry pair compositions can identify state-specific frequencies through the convolution. But if **every** carrier has pair (A,B), `(p_A,p_B)=(.9,.1)` and `(.1,.9)` give identical genotype distributions `[.09,.82,.09]`. The sign of the change in the named A state is then not identified. Phase or enough AA/BB anchors is needed. Ancestry counts do not establish H1–A versus H2–B linkage; AMY ordered-looking H1/H2 table columns are not linked SNP haplotypes.

[inference] Misassignment mixes true state frequencies: with assignment matrix `M_{r,s}(t,pop)`, an observed called-r frequency is a composition-weighted mixture of `p_s(t,pop)`. If error varies with date, ancestry, coverage or imputation reference, it can create a conditional slope when every true slope is zero. Constant nondifferential error can attenuate/dilute or mix opposite slopes. Neither hard thresholds nor soft weights correct misspecified error posteriors automatically. Reference leave-one-population-out tests, calibrated mosaics and quality-stratified residual checks are necessary.

[inference] Excluding the focal genotype removes **direct** circularity, not biological LD or imputation leakage. Nearby markers can remain linked to the selected allele; an imputation algorithm that used the focal region can propagate its information into retained flanks. Conversely, a 412 kb exclusion can span a recombination/state boundary: agreement of left/right ancestry does not establish the fine state inside the gap. Use a genetic map and validated posterior bridging; abstain if bridge uncertainty is large. Changing forbidden input alleles must leave assignments identical at a fixed preprocessing boundary, but that software test alone is not locus independence of the original imputation.

[inference] Even perfectly conditioning on a declared source state removes only **between-declared-state composition change**. Migration or turnover of finer lineages inside that state, drift, sampling, selection on linked variants and ancestry assignment errors remain possible explanations. Conditioning very finely can also absorb the selected lineage itself or make the allele invariant. Shared states are shared ancestry, not independent mutation origins; several populations carrying the same selected lineage are not several independent replications.

## 5. Recommendation and constraint verdict

[inference] For **coarse source-conditioned frequencies**, the best immediate phase-free *tool pipeline candidate* is real **ELAI upper-state pair posteriors (`--ps2`) + ASAFE-type temporal likelihood**, using adequate imputed/quality-checked sources rather than sparse raw AIM calls. With validated phased/QC input, **RFMix plus an independent Gnomix check** has stronger directly ancient empirical support. Neither source labeling alone meets the finer local ancestral-haplotype/“recent ancestor” reading of the objective.

[inference] For the specified **local sharing-state** objective, choose a **real linked haplotype model, ELAI/fastPHASE lower-state pair inference, plus the ASAFE-type unphased temporal likelihood** as a conditional research design. It needs lower-state joint posteriors/bridge uncertainty exported from the real model, calibrated on ancestry-matched mosaics and imputation uncertainty. This avoids fabricating phased haplotypes or reusing all-SNP k-means. Lower states remain operational ancestral-sharing proxies, not MRCA identities. If actual genealogical ancestors are required, obtain validated phase and infer local trees (Relate/tsinfer/path ancestry); that is a different, substantial pipeline.

[observation] **No validated, off-the-shelf implementation of the exact fine-state pipeline is available in this session.** The official ELAI standard joint output is S×S upper ancestry; it is NOT K×K lower sharing-state output. The full bundle is NoQC and the loader does not expose per-genotype uncertainty/phase. The small structural cohort lacks phase linkage to the flanks. These are missing inputs/interfaces/validation, not proof that all unphased statistical inference is impossible. Downloading an ELAI binary is small; software installation cost alone is NOT the scientific blocker. No biological frequency statistic will be run here.

[inference] Required next pipeline: (1) recover genotype probabilities/INFO and imputation provenance or ancestry-matched downsampled truth; (2) mask the entire corrected AMY exclusion before any new phase/imputation and assert orientation; (3) use authentic linked-model lower-pair output, checking the S=1 implementation/export against enumeration and simulated linked diplotypes; (4) train one outcome-blind dictionary with explicit family/quality/sample weights; (5) pass the preregistered structure, local truth, stability, coverage, leakage, null and power gates; (6) only then estimate state/population temporal frequency paths and report covariance-discounted shared-state concordance. No substitution of upper-state dosage regression for step (6).
