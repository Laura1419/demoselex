# Independent re-derivation of the amylase argument

## Scope and logical requirements — written before new results

[observation] 2026-10-08T19:57Z. Read MISSION, capstone, decisions, Akbari reconstruction, both I analyses, subset/trend preregistrations and results, simulation calibration/power, trajectory validation/merge/relatedness, AMY loader/report and Lean sources/ledger. New numerical results are appended below, not substituted for earlier authors' conclusions.

[inference] **Consistent directional change can be evidence for selection, but demography does not universally predict arbitrary independent signs.** The valid claim is conditional: independently evolving units, measured over their OWN durations, repeatedly increase a prespecified functional state more often/more strongly than a specified neutral demographic process predicts. Selection need not outperform another scan for this argument to hold. Conversely, a rare path under a narrow null does not identify selection against every possible demographic history.

[inference] Separate two observations that the quotation conflates: (1) each deme increases internally at multiple time points; (2) successive replacing demes arrive with progressively higher means, even if each stays flat. The first uses independent within-deme innovations. The second needs source-population sampling/ancestry information: the replacing demes may inherit earlier increases, share donors, or be ordered by correlated origin. One high-copy source progressively mixing into several recipient demes produces the same-direction pattern without new selection in those recipients. Neither splitting nor merging correlated population profiles creates new independent demographic innovations.

[inference] Under independent FAIR nonzero directions, D demes all moving in either direction has P=2*(1/2)^D=2^(1-D). If increasing was prespecified, P=2^(-D). For D=2,3,4,5,6,10 the two-sided probabilities are .5,.25,.125,.0625,.03125,.001953125; D=4-5 is not 'incredibly implausible.' At least D=6 is needed for a two-sided pure-sign .05 test even with perfect direction estimation. Independence and fairness, not simply 'demography,' do the work. Near a frequency/copy-number lower bound, neutral changes need not have symmetric signs, and shared innovations substantially increase concordance. A fractional m^2/sum(R) is NOT an exact count that can be placed in 2^(1-D).

[inference] Ordered independent continuously distributed deme MEANS are a different experiment: one prespecified strict ordering has probability 1/D!, or 2/D! for either monotone orientation. Signs of consecutive mean differences are NOT independent (they share the middle mean). If instead these are independent increments of a lineage, the fair-increment 2^(1-D) formula counts D increments, not D sampled means. Shared descent and geographic donor gradients violate iid mean exchangeability. Distinguish these nulls before quoting a probability for the author's 'ordered-deme' argument.

## Did the earlier project test that argument?

[observation] The earlier within-trajectory scans estimate slopes, binomial errors and relatedness-discounted pooled scores; their primary reference is the distribution of that score at other matched loci. The subset test maximizes over subsets and uses the same max at control loci. The trend lane regresses six history means on their average dates and compares permutation/matched-locus statistics. It is NOT an analysis of AMY1 copy number across independently rebuilt demes.

[inference] Matched loci can be an excellent empirical check on a demographic process. They are not intrinsically the wrong null: conditional exchangeability can yield valid locus-outlier tests without explicit Ne. But asking 'is this locus unusual versus the background?' is different from evaluating the probability of sustained directional replication under a drift-and-migration law. A leave-one-out rank pass fraction of about .05 is partly a rank construction and cannot independently prove that drift/admixture were correctly modeled. Background low-Akbari loci may also contain selection.

[observation] The v1 giant component spanned 0–65,000 BP (9,648 individuals). The v3 10,058-person G0 actually spans **100–8,850 BP**, NOT the entire 65,000-year record. It still mixes locations and ancestry/period clusters and is not one deme. The capstone itself acknowledges this. The trajectory merge uses transitive ancestry similarity rather than geographically anchored genetic continuity.

[inference] **The capstone's 'falsified' and 'not present' conclusions are too strong.** No detections with coarse finite-control P-value tails, imperfect units, and no copy-number test do not falsify the accepted directional-AMY argument. Reported agreement with Akbari and lack of unusual scores remain observations about those panels/statistics, not a proof that the directional argument fails. Likewise, ancestry-collinear time and 'carried-in change' are NOT the same physical situation: selection within admixing populations can be correlated with ancestry history. Their blind spots can overlap, but equality is not established by these analyses.

[observation] Lean Replacement.lean explicitly ASSUMES independent fair observed signs in signPairMass(1/2). The proof does not derive fairness/independence from population replacement. Statistic.lean's duplication invariance assumes a supplied genealogical family mapping; the numerical matrix cannot infer that mapping by theorem. These are correct conditional statements, not a demographic identification theorem. No Lean source is changed in this session.

## Correct statistic and null

[inference] Define units without AMY or candidate locus information, enforce >=3 nodes and substantial duration, estimate signed frequency/CN slope within each unit's own time series, and report fitted change, uncertainty, signs and ancestry overlap. For SNPs a coherent null has latent p(t) innovations with Wright–Fisher variance p(1-p)*delta_generations/(2Ne), donor-specific admixture jumps m*(p_donor-p), sampling variance, and shared innovation covariance. An exact donor model is preferable to centered jump variance when source frequencies/ancestry proportions can be estimated. The preregistered bounded implementation estimates random-walk plus centered jump second moments on independent background loci and tests a standardized sum of per-deme slopes; it preserves the cumulative temporal covariance rather than pretending time bins are independent.

[inference] Calibrate on HELD-OUT background loci matched by support/time design and frequency standardization, not the training loci or a post-hoc rank remapping. Require the .05 rate to lie inside its binomial interval with adequate sample count; report conditional power before biological interpretation. Failure blocks inference and calls for a newly preregistered model, not a selected tail probability.

[inference] Copy number is a quantitative, recurrently mutating structural trait. SNP Ne does not specify CN diffusion: need structural haplotype frequencies, their copy counts, mutation law, donor composition and measurement noise. Without those, the probability of a CN ordering under 'drift plus jumps' is not uniquely identified. A fair-sign reference may be descriptive but is not that process probability. Archaeological starch association and independent high-copy mutational backgrounds could strengthen the author's case on different axes; this session has not independently verified the Andes claim or its mutation-level independence.

## Two further audit corrections — appended after source cross-check

[observation] docs/traj-trend-prereg.md §4a/§5 specifies permutation P as PRIMARY; docs/traj-trend-results.md instead calls matched-control P the preregistered primary after reporting permutation miscalibration (8.2%). [inference] Choosing the better calibrated reference is scientifically sensible but is a protocol change, not the unchanged preregistered decision rule as described. Without a dated pre-result amendment, that comparison should be treated as exploratory. Miscalibration is a model problem, not a falsifier of a biological selection hypothesis.

[inference] The earlier 'BH-FDR unreachable by construction' assertion is also not generally correct. BH tests `p_(k) <= alpha*k/m`, not only alpha/m. A coarse finite-control minimum P prevents isolated discoveries but can allow sufficiently MANY small P-values. For m=3,000, alpha=.05 and minimum P~.0024, roughly 144 discoveries at that floor could pass. At m=8,000 and P_min=.0065, roughly 1,040 would be needed. The observed lack of discoveries remains an observation; the rank-dependent threshold and attainable power must be analyzed before claiming impossibility or biological falsification.

[inference] A deterministic demographic counterexample is now tested: six constant resident sources with frequencies .1,.2,.3,.4,.5,.6, one constant donor with frequency .8, and increasing admixture fractions 0,.2,.4 produce six positive within-region slopes with NO change in any source frequency. This disproves the unrestricted statement that demography must give arbitrary signs. To make the accepted argument causal, establish independent innovations or account for these donor contributions; molecular independence and independent continents may supply evidence that a single regional admixture mechanism cannot.

## Direct directional-statistic refinement (secondary, preregistered before its run)

[inference] The initial Gaussian sum detects an average trend but can be driven by one strong deme, so it alone does not establish consistent direction. Amendment 4 adds the weakest-replicate statistic `T=max(min Z,min(-Z))` under the same fitted drift-plus-jump law. This directly measures the least agreeing replicate, and records all signs; it is secondary/exploratory because the original sum outcomes had already been inspected.

[observation] Independent held-out control-locus pass fraction for this directional statistic **9/208=4.33%**, 95% interval **2.00–8.05%**; its preliminary calibration gate passes. Conditional common-effect power .25/.5/1/2 = **7.53/15.27/42.77/90.22%**. The .05 threshold T=-.119 allows weak near-zero disagreement, whereas T>0 denotes literal unanimity. [inference] This is a better match to the author's replication argument than an outlier-dominated average, but neither it nor SNP calibration can overcome D_CN=1 or supply a calibrated CN ordering probability.
