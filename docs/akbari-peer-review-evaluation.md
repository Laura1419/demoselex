## 3. Deep dive: topics bearing on this project

### (a) Population structure, admixture, GRM, migration, and loss of real selection


[observation] **Turnover artifacts were explicitly raised.** “In some cases, outlier variants between populations may indeed result from past selection, but the time and place of that selection would be far removed from what is reported by the model.” (V0, R2, PDF p. 4) R1 independently asked for “loci where a positively selected variant in source populations becomes neutral after admixture” (V0, R1, PDF p. 2). This is a distinction between source selection and selection during the sampled transect, not a claim that all carried changes are biologically irrelevant.

[observation] **Time–GRM collinearity was explicitly raised.** “Therefore, the question is not whether including the kinship matrix in the GLMM results in the inclusion of covariates collinear with the time axis (it surely does), but whether the GLMM correctly estimates how much variation there should be along these axes in the absence of selection.” (V0, R3, PDF p. 5) The response emphasized GLMM-versus-PC power simulations and “we constrain the σ2 parameter to be no smaller than its mean value under the null for the corresponding MAF bin” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 40). [inference] This floor is a false-positive safeguard; it does not remove ancestry-collinear directions from the random effect or prove recovery of selection aligned with them.

[observation] **No referee explicitly asks whether the GRM removes real ancestry-aligned selection or demands a false-negative analysis in that direction.** The nearest comments concern time–GRM collinearity, correct null variance, equal-sample-size power, fluctuating-selection false negatives and pre-admixture selection. Authors themselves concede “the GLMM may not perform better than other methods, since much of the signal can be absorbed into the variance component.” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 23) (the example is a panmictic two-time-point sample). This is not an AMY finding or a demonstrated over-correction class.

[observation] **Later authors explicitly acknowledge selection on ancestry-associated axes.** Against the requested residual-ancestry diagnostic, they say “Variants that are strongly associated with ancestry components whose proportions change over time are not expected to be neutral, even under correct ancestry correction, when selection acts on standing variation or along ancestry-associated axes.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 59) They continue “this correlation does not imply incomplete correction.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 59) Their stated goal is “to correct for it rigorously without sacrificing excessive power.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 59) [inference] This anticipates selection–ancestry entanglement, but not the project’s quantitative information-loss result. It validly challenges ancestry association as a sufficient confounding diagnostic; it does not prove either complete correction or zero power cost.

[observation] The response to migration/source selection ultimately states “our GLMM does not detect pre-admixture selection.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 48) and “Under the significance threshold |X| > 5.45, the false-positive rate in this experiment is zero.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 48) [inference] Here source-only selection is counted as a negative control **for current-transect selection**, not an evolutionary process the scan intends to recover. That estimand distinction is central to our carriage framing. The reported zero is finite-experiment evidence as asserted by the authors, not a universal guarantee.


### (b) |X| > 5.45, FDR, and a non-normal null


[observation] **The threshold was heavily challenged, but primarily as too permissive or inadequately justified, not too stringent for candidate prioritization.** R2’s initial concern is “using a loose significance cutoff.” (V0, R2, PDF p. 4) R3 writes “I don't think there is enough evidence to overwrite the existing statistical significance standards in this field.” (V0, R3, PDF p. 6) V2 R1 asks for “the TPR and FPR of the GLMM and GLM models under models 1 to 3 considering |X| > 5.45 as the significance threshold” (V2, R1, PDF p. 7). The same report gives “|X| > 5.45, corresponding to P-value < 1.95 × 10-16” (V2, R1, PDF p. 7). These are reviewers’ manuscript descriptions, not our adoption of X as a standard-normal score.

[observation] **The authors do not deny null misbehavior.** “SNP ascertainment in the imputation reference panel, together with the non-linear logit transformation in generalized linear (mixed) models, generates an allele frequency-dependent bias and inflation.” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 41) Their conclusion is “we cannot use the bulk of the distribution to calibrate the tail of the distribution.” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 37) [inference] A rescaling of X does not establish a Gaussian null; the authors instead rely on functional-proxy enrichment.

[observation] **The eventual FDR rationale explicitly makes an ordering assumption:** “variants with larger absolute values of the selection statistic are increasingly enriched for true signals of directional selection, implying a monotonic decrease in the false discovery rate as the statistic increases.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 57) They also state “the observed plateau in enrichment identifies a regime in which the contribution of false positives is negligible.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 57) [inference] A plateau is not by itself an identified zero-FDR tail: for annotation A, P(A|tail) = (1−FDR)P(A|true,tail) + FDR P(A|null,tail). Without assumptions about both conditional annotation rates, a constant mixture can plateau with nonzero FDR; changing true-signal annotation prevalence can also plateau. The simulations check specified couplings, not every possible coupling. This is a limitation of inference from enrichment, not evidence that their empirical threshold is wrong.

[observation] In response to threshold-matched validation, authors report “TP values of 26.5%, 59.25%, 89%, and 22.25% for models 1, 2.1, 2.2, and 3, respectively, and FP equal to zero for all models.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 48) [inference] This is directly responsive to the operating-point request. It does not determine the missed-locus burden in our real subthreshold band or give each band SNP a causal selection probability.

[observation] **Independent calibration from siblings was requested and declined:** “Can the authors repeat their enrichment analysis using sibling-based GWAS statistics?” (V0, R1, PDF p. 2) Answer: “While sibling-based GWAS may offer high specificity, it suffers from severe power limitations due to much smaller sample sizes, reducing sensitivity.” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 17) followed by “We thus continue to report enrichment analyses only for population-based GWAS.” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 17) [inference] Family-GWAS replication of a polygenic trait cannot be substituted for this declined threshold-calibration analysis.

[observation] **Magnitude and interpretation changed over the exchange.** R3 described enrichment “approximately 3.9” (V0, R3, PDF p. 6) and background-adjusted “approximately 2.1” (V0, R3, PDF p. 6). Authors initially say “The magnitude of the enrichment is not a concern” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 43) and use “15–20%” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 43) for GWAS-hit prevalence. V2 R2 emphasizes “the 5-fold enrichment that the authors report here is very impressive” (V2, R2, PDF p. 9); authors subsequently describe the annotation as “approximately 12.5% of the variants analyzed here.” (V2 response, authors, PDF p. 58) V3 response quotes adjusted enrichment “~2.4-times” (V3 response, authors, PDF p. 61). [inference] These are version-specific assertions (2.1 versus 2.4; 15–20% versus 12.5%), not interchangeable independent measurements. [observation] **Neither the 3.16 boundary nor an FDR-50% candidate band appears in the extracted review/rebuttal prose.** No referee requests a threshold-sensitivity recall analysis, systematic follow-up of that band, or an explicit phenotype-prioritization utility calculation. ### (e) Selection–demography correlation, parallel/repeated selection, drift identifiability


[observation] Ancestry-correlated true selection is explicit in the authors’ V2 reply (p. 59, quoted in §3a); source-only selection and null drift simulation are explicit in V0 reports (pp. 2, 4–6). **The terms parallel selection and repeated selection do not appear, and no report requests a test of repeated within-lineage change.** The related concept appears in authors’ explanation: “If selection pressures are broadly consistent across space and time, heterogeneity provides natural replicates of the evolutionary experiment, enabling the GLMM to integrate signals across datasets with fewer degrees of freedom.” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 23) They immediately qualify “this assumption does not capture cases where selection differs strongly across space” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 23) or time. [inference] This anticipates the homogeneous-versus-heterogeneous selection distinction, not our empirical result or a demonstration that those natural replicates are independent.

[observation] Authors also invoke shared-founder drift/linked-selection timescales: “convergent correlations across replicates, that is, the correlation between allele frequency changes of two replicates over the same time interval, also decays rapidly after 10–20 generations of evolution from the founder population.” (V0 response, authors, PDF p. 34) [inference] This is their interpretation of Buffalo and Coop, not a computed effective-history count or calibrated genealogy for our demes. It cannot establish that ancestry-labelled populations are independent replicates.

[observation] **There is no explicit identifiability theorem, quantitative ancestry-collinear information-loss curve, or within/carriage decomposition in the review file.** The closest reviewer questions are correct null variance on the time axis (V0 R3 p. 5) and mixture composition versus outliers (V0 R2 p. 5). [inference] Treating those processes together does not identify a selected-versus-drift source history or estimate how much genuine carried selection the correction misses.
