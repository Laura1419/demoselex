## Novelty statement

This literature survey reveals that while numerous methods exist for detecting selection in structured populations, most approaches have significant limitations for our specific problem of reconstructing adaptation across complex migration and admixture histories:

**What is solved:** Existing methods like Simon & Coop's decomposition framework, CLUES with ancestry stratification, PolyGraph/GRoSS with admixture graphs, and trajectory inference methods provide key building blocks for analyzing selection in structured populations with temporal data. They collectively address the need to separate selection from drift/migration, account for population structure, and analyze temporal dynamics.

**What remains open:** No single method comprehensively addresses the full scope of our problem: simultaneously (1) decomposing drift/migration/selection contributions, (2) handling complex admixture histories, (3) analyzing temporal allele frequency trajectories, (4) detecting repeated adaptive responses across independent historical episodes, and (5) identifying population replacement vs continuity without archaeological priors.

The key gap is the integration of temporal trajectory analysis with ancestry-aware selection inference and cross-historical pattern detection. Methods like Simon & Coop provide the decomposition framework, but need extension to handle the full complexity of ancient DNA time series with admixture. Similarly, admixture graph methods like PolyGraph need temporal dimension integration. Our project will need to draw from multiple methodological strands to develop a comprehensive approach for reconstructing adaptation across complex population histories.

**Critical missing capability:** The ability to systematically compare adaptive responses across independent but similar historical scenarios (e.g., adaptation to farming transitions in different regions) with quantitative measures of the consistency/directionality of selection, while accounting for differences in demographic history and sampling.
